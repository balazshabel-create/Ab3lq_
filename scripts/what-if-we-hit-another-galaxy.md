# What If We Hit Another Galaxy?

*Short-form "what if" script: 3D-animated explainer, vertical 9:16, about 70 seconds.*

| # | Visual | Narration (VO) |
|---|--------|----------------|
| 1 | Night. A guy is eating cereal on his porch. Behind him, a gigantic spiral galaxy fills the sky. | "This is what the night sky would look like if our galaxy crashed into another one." |
| 2 | Zoom out from Earth until the whole Milky Way spiral is visible, with a "YOU ARE HERE" tag on a tiny dot. | "Our galaxy, the Milky Way, has hundreds of billions of stars, and our Sun is just one of them." |
| 3 | Two spiral galaxies in space. The second one, labeled "ANDROMEDA", is drifting toward us. "110 KM/S" pops up. | "And it's not alone. Our neighbor, Andromeda, is heading toward us at about 110 kilometers per second." |
| 4 | A giant gold coin flips in front of the two galaxies. "50/50?" | "But scientists aren't sure they'll actually hit. Some studies call it a coin flip. Others think it's more likely." |
| 5 | Back on the porch, time-lapse: Andromeda grows bigger and bigger in the night sky while a counter ticks up in billions of years. | "If it happens, Andromeda would slowly grow in our sky over billions of years, until it fills the whole night." |
| 6 | Wide shot: the two galaxies swing past each other and long tails of stars peel off. | "But here's the crazy part. The stars themselves would almost never crash into each other." |
| 7 | A single glowing grain of sand labeled "SUN" on an empty desert. The camera rises until another grain, "30 KM AWAY", appears. | "If the Sun were a grain of sand, the next closest star would be about thirty kilometers away." |
| 8 | Close on the collision: clouds glow pink as new stars ignite. | "Instead, giant clouds of gas would slam together and light up with billions of brand-new stars." |
| 9 | One star tagged "SUN" gets dragged out along a tidal tail. | "Our solar system could get flung way out into a totally new part of the galaxy." |
| 10 | Two black holes with glowing rings spiral together. Flash. | "Eventually, the giant black holes at their centers would spiral together and merge." |
| 11 | The merged galaxy, a huge glowing blob. "MILKOMEDA". | "Leaving one huge, blobby galaxy that scientists nicknamed Milkomeda." |
| 12 | Back on the porch: the sky is orange and blazing, the guy is sweating. He shrugs and keeps eating. | "And honestly? By then, the Sun will probably have made Earth way too hot for us anyway." |
| 13 | Loop: night sky, the giant galaxy fades in again. | *(Low boom, back to scene 1.)* |

---

## Production notes

- **Hook (0–3s):** The galaxy filling the night sky has to be the very first frame.
- **Pacing:** One idea per shot, cut on every sentence.
- **Sound:** A low drone throughout. A boom on the first pass, then a riser into the black-hole merger.
- **Loop:** Scene 13 cuts back into scene 1.

## The real science behind it

- **Andromeda is coming:** Andromeda (M31) is approaching the Milky Way at roughly 110 km/s relative to our galaxy's center.
- **The odds aren't settled:** A 2025 study using Hubble and Gaia data (Sawala et al.) found only about a 50/50 chance of a merger within 10 billion years, and roughly a 2% chance of a head-on hit in 4–5 billion years. A later study suggests a merger is more likely than that. The narration deliberately avoids a single number.
- **Stars don't collide:** Galaxies are mostly empty space. If the Sun were a 1 mm grain of sand, the nearest star (Proxima Centauri) would be about 30 km away. Gas clouds *do* collide, which triggers bursts of star formation.
- **Tidal tails and flinging:** Gravity during the close passes pulls long streams of stars out of both galaxies. The Sun could end up much farther from the center.
- **Black holes:** The central supermassive black holes would sink to the middle and eventually merge.
- **"Milkomeda":** This is the informal nickname for the merged galaxy, expected to be a large elliptical galaxy.
- **The Sun first:** In about a billion years the Sun will be bright enough to start boiling away Earth's oceans, well before any collision. That's why the narration hedges with "probably".
- **Caveat:** The video's collision is a simplified gravity simulation, not an exact model of our galaxies.
