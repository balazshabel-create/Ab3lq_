# What If We Hit Another Universe?

*Short-form "what if" script: 3D-animated explainer, vertical 9:16, about 60 seconds.*

| # | Visual | Narration (VO) |
|---|--------|----------------|
| 1 | A guy is eating cereal on his porch. The sky behind him **cracks** like glass. | "This is what it would look like if our universe crashed into another one." |
| 2 | Zoom out past Earth, the galaxy and the whole observable universe, until it's a single glowing soap bubble floating in a dark foam full of other bubbles. | "Some physicists think our universe is just one bubble in a giant cosmic foam, called the multiverse." |
| 3 | The bubbles keep inflating. Two of them drift together. | "And these bubbles are constantly growing. So every now and then, two of them could touch." |
| 4 | Back on the porch: a calm, sunny day. A faint, impossibly straight line appears on the horizon. | "If one hit us, we wouldn't see it coming, because the edge of the other universe would be moving at almost the speed of light." |
| 5 | Split screen. Left: our universe, labeled with gravity, electrons and light. Right: the other one, with the symbols glitched and scrambled. | "The scary part is that the other universe might run on completely different laws of physics." |
| 6 | The wall sweeps across a city. A tree passes through it and **unravels** into shimmering dust, atom by atom. | "If its physics is more stable than ours, its bubble would just keep expanding into our space, rewriting everything it touched." |
| 7 | X-ray view of a hand as the wall passes: the atoms fall apart because electrons can't hold on anymore. | "Atoms might not be able to hold together. Chemistry, stars and people would just stop working." |
| 8 | The wall reaches the cereal guy mid-bite. Freeze frame. | "And the whole thing would take less than a second. Nobody would feel a thing." |
| 9 | Hard cut to a scientist staring at a blotchy map of the cosmic microwave background, circling one cold spot. | "But there's a less scary version. If the collision happened long ago and far away, it might have just left a bruise on the sky." |
| 10 | The circled spot pulses. The text "Cold Spot?" appears. | "Some scientists have actually searched the oldest light in the universe for these cosmic bruises. So far, nothing confirmed." |
| 11 | Back to the cereal guy. The sky is normal. He shrugs and keeps eating. | "So if another universe hits us, we probably won't know until it's already happened." |
| 12 | Loop: the sky starts to crack again. | *(Silence, then the crack sound, looping back to scene 1.)* |

---

## Production notes

- **Hook (0–3s):** The cracking sky has to land in the first frame. Don't open on a title.
- **Pacing:** One idea per shot, 4–6 seconds each. Cut on every sentence.
- **Sound:** A low hum that builds as the wall approaches, then total silence at the freeze frame (scene 8).
- **Loop:** Scene 12 cuts straight back into scene 1 so the video replays seamlessly.

## The real science behind it

- **Bubble universes:** The idea comes from *eternal inflation*, where inflating space keeps forming separate "bubble" universes that can each have their own physical constants.
- **Collisions:** Two bubbles can collide when they nucleate close together. A collision in our distant past could have left a circular pattern in the cosmic microwave background (CMB).
- **The search:** Researchers have looked for these patterns in WMAP and Planck data. Nothing statistically significant has been found yet. The CMB "Cold Spot" gets mentioned as a candidate, but it's speculative.
- **The doom scenario:** A bubble with lower vacuum energy expanding into ours works like *vacuum decay*. Its wall would move at nearly light speed and change the laws of physics as it went.
- **Caveat:** None of this is confirmed. The multiverse is a hypothesis, not established science, and the narration should keep its "some physicists think" framing.
